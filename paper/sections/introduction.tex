\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) has become the
dominant recipe for reasoning models: o1-style systems and DeepSeek-R1
\citep{deepseekr1} optimize the language model's own weights with a
rule-checkable reward so that the model emits a better chain of thought,
token by token, over a flat and growing context. This paper studies a
complementary design point in which \emph{none of the language model's
weights ever change}. Instead, a small external controller network, on the
order of a few million parameters, learns to orchestrate a frozen language
model by issuing typed operations over a highly structured reasoning state.

The system rests on four commitments. First, the \textbf{state} is not a
transcript of tokens but an immutable, nested reasoning forest: goals and
optional subgoals, tentative hypotheses, facts carrying provenance
(\texttt{given}, \texttt{derived}, or \texttt{verified}), and a log of
verifier calls. Second, each \textbf{action} is a pair
$(\textit{verb}, \textit{target})$: one of six typed operations
(\textsc{Split}, \textsc{FormHypothesis}, \textsc{ComputeSubexpr},
\textsc{Verify}, \textsc{Commit}, \textsc{Abandon}) applied to a specific
node of the forest. Each verb is a \emph{lens}: it renders only a
grounded-local slice of the state (the problem, the ancestor path, and the
target's own subtree) into the frozen model's context, and writes the
model's schema-constrained output back into that node's neighborhood. Third,
the only source of \textbf{reward} is a symbolic verifier: a terminal
verdict when the root goal is committed, and nothing else; there is no
reward model, no learned process reward, and no LLM judge anywhere in the
reward path. Fourth, \textbf{training} is off-policy value-based
Q-learning over sampled transitions, with Monte Carlo returns from the
verifier terminal; Monte Carlo Tree Search is not the training spine but an
optional, strictly additive accelerator (better exploration and better
regression targets) and a test-time compute knob.

Why should the structure help? A small language model reasons poorly across
a long, flat context but well over a short, on-topic one. The lens exploits
exactly this: when the controller acts on a subgoal, the frozen model is
shown that subgoal's small neighborhood rather than the entire history. The
nested state is the substrate that makes such focusing possible, and the
verifier is what keeps the learning signal honest. The deployment claim is
correspondingly a \emph{compute-frontier} claim, not a raw-accuracy claim:
a few-million-parameter controller choosing actions at search budget
$B{=}0$, against baselines that spend an LLM forward pass (or many) per
decision (\cref{sec:experimental-setup}). One honesty note attaches to
that claim: the controller's heads are cheap, but its \emph{encoder} need
not be. Under the first-version encoder (the frozen LLM's own hidden
states at marker positions), every decision still costs one LLM prefill to
embed the state; the per-decision cost becomes negligible only under the
later graph-encoder rung (\cref{sec:method-controller}). The frontier
claim is therefore made per encoder rung, never blended across them.

\paragraph{Contributions.} No single element below is new on its own
(\cref{sec:related}); the contribution is the conjunction, and an
architecture that realizes it end to end:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=0pt]
\item a formulation of LLM deliberation as a generative MDP over a
  structured reasoning forest, with typed $(\textit{verb}, \textit{target})$
  actions realized as lenses (scoped read, localized write) over a frozen
  generator;
\item a tiny external controller with a factored policy: a categorical verb
  head and a pointer head over the live node identifiers of a growing
  forest, plus a Q head, trained by off-policy value-based Q-learning with a
  symbolic verifier as the sole reward;
\item a three-rung training ladder (LLM-as-controller, behavior-cloning
  warm start, RL fine-tune) that never asks the network to learn from
  scratch under sparse terminal reward, with MCTS as a strictly additive
  option rather than a requirement;
\item a pre-registered compute-versus-accuracy evaluation protocol on
  verifiable math benchmarks with FLOP-matched sampling and orchestration
  baselines. \stub{empirical contribution line, pending runs}
\end{enumerate}
