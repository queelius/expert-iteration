\begin{abstract}
The framework paper at \texttt{paper/} presents an approximate $Q$-learning
system over an LLM-induced context MDP with verifier-anchored reward,
optionally using Monte Carlo Tree Search as a policy improvement operator
in the expert-iteration sense. Within that framework, intermediate
deliberation states are themselves tree-structured: a hypothesis may split
into sub-hypotheses, a subgoal may have nested sub-subgoals, and a
verifier call may follow up on an earlier one. The framework paper
introduces these structural possibilities as forward-compatibility hooks
via stable identifiers and implicit parent-pointer references, then
defers their exercise. The present paper takes that activation seriously.
We argue that structural recursion in deliberation has two qualitatively
different modes. Productive recursion is structural decomposition: a
tentative claim splits into sub-claims, each addressing a
verifier-checkable sub-problem, so terminal reward reaches the leaves of
the implicit tree and the approximate $Q$-function can credit-assign
across the structure. Unproductive recursion is meta-criticism: a
critique of a critique of a critique, structurally legal but receiving
no terminal verifier signal beneath. The two modes share an action space
and a state schema, yet one is the engine of compositional reasoning
while the other consumes sample budget without yielding learning signal.
We develop three design principles: that a bounded depth limit acts as
a structural prior suppressing the unproductive mode without foreclosing
the productive one; that identifier-based action targeting extends the
framework's seven-verb action set to arbitrary tree depth with no new
categorical actions; and that serializing the implicit tree as flat
tokens with depth markers lets a frozen language model's self-attention
resolve recursive structure without graph neural networks or
tree-LSTMs. We pre-register an ablation that varies the depth limit
across zero, three, and unbounded settings, holding the framework
paper's benchmarks and base models fixed.
\end{abstract}
