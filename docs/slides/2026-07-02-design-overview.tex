% ---------------------------------------------------------------- overview
\begin{frame}{What this is (and is not)}
  \begin{block}{The system}
    A \alert{tiny external controller} (verb head + pointer head + $Q$ head,
    a few million parameters) learns to orchestrate a \alert{frozen} large
    language model (LLM) by issuing typed $(\text{verb}, \text{target})$
    actions over a structured, nested reasoning forest. A \alert{symbolic
    verifier} is the only reward source.
  \end{block}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Not} o1 / DeepSeek-R1: reinforcement learning never
          touches the LLM's weights
    \item \textbf{Not} a learned reward model: no process or outcome reward
          model, no critic, no LLM-as-judge anywhere in the reward path
    \item Training spine: off-policy value-based $Q$-learning; Monte Carlo
          Tree Search (MCTS) is a strictly additive accelerator and the
          test-time compute knob
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{State: a nested AND-OR reasoning forest}
  \centering
  \begin{tikzpicture}[
      level distance=13mm,
      level 1/.style={sibling distance=62mm},
      level 2/.style={sibling distance=42mm},
      g/.style={draw, rounded corners=2pt, align=center, font=\scriptsize,
                inner sep=3pt, fill=green!10},
      f/.style={draw, align=center, font=\scriptsize, inner sep=3pt, fill=blue!8},
      dead/.style={draw, align=center, font=\scriptsize, inner sep=3pt,
                   fill=gray!20, text=gray},
      edge from parent/.style={draw, -{Stealth[length=1.5mm]}}]
    \node[g] {Goal: solve $|x-2| = 5$ \; \scriptsize(connective: exhaustive\_case)}
      child {node[g] {Case $x \ge 2$:\\ $x - 2 = 5$}
        child {node[f] {Fact: $5 + 2 = 7$ \;(verified)}}
      }
      child {node[g] {Case $x < 2$:\\ $2 - x = 5$}
        child {node[f] {Fact: $2 - 5 = -3$ \;(verified)}}
        child {node[dead] {Hyp: $x = 3$\\ (abandoned)}}
      };
  \end{tikzpicture}

  \medskip
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Frozen dataclasses: \texttt{Goal}, \texttt{Hypothesis},
          \texttt{Fact}, \texttt{VerifierCall}; verbs are total functions
          $\text{Graph} \to \text{Ok} \mid \text{Err}$
    \item No reward or value stored in nodes; \texttt{solved}/\texttt{verified}
          are derived predicates
    \item Structure never gates: the connective is metadata for the
          controller, not a hard rule
    \item Closed subtrees compact to one line in every rendered view
          (built-in roll-back / summarize)
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- actions
\begin{frame}{Action space: six verbs, one pointer}
  \begin{center}
    \footnotesize
    \begin{tabular}{lll}
      \toprule
      Verb & LLM call? & Effect \\
      \midrule
      \verbword{split} & yes & decompose a goal into subgoals or exhaustive cases \\
      \verbword{form\_hypothesis} & yes & add a tentative claim under a goal \\
      \verbword{compute} & yes & add one checkable ground equation as a fact \\
      \verbword{verify} & \alert{no} & run the symbolic checker on a node's statement \\
      \verbword{commit} & \alert{no} & close a goal on verifier-vouched evidence \\
      \verbword{abandon} & \alert{no} & prune a dead end (excluded from menus and views) \\
      \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
  \end{center}
  \begin{block}{The lens property}
    Choosing $(\text{verb}, \text{target})$ simultaneously picks the
    operation \emph{and} the slice of state the frozen generator sees:
    problem + ancestor path + target subtree, siblings hidden, closed
    subtrees compacted. Write-back is local and sanitized against marker
    injection.
  \end{block}
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- reward + training
\begin{frame}{Reward invariant and the training ladder}
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
      \textbf{Reward (load-bearing)}
      \begin{itemize}
        \item Terminal $+1/-1$ at root \verbword{commit}; $0$ elsewhere
        \item Masked/illegal action: small penalty
        \item No learned reward of any kind, ever
        \item Aesthetic signals may enter only as search priors, never as
              Bellman targets
      \end{itemize}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
      \textbf{Three rungs}
      \begin{enumerate}
        \item \alert{LLM-as-controller}: a strong teacher picks
              $(\text{verb},\text{target})$; trajectories logged as
              JSON lines
        \item Behavior cloning: warm-start $\pitheta$ on rung-1 data
        \item $Q$-learning fine-tune: off-policy, n-step / Monte Carlo
              returns from the verifier; MCTS optional for better targets
      \end{enumerate}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
  \medskip
  \footnotesize
  Design-true collection regime: a strong \emph{hosted} teacher model
  (reached over a network endpoint) decides; a \emph{local small generator}
  writes all content, so trajectories reflect the deployed model's state
  distribution.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Prior art: the landscape}
  \footnotesize
  \begin{center}

      \toprule

      \midrule
      Search + train the LLM & TS-LLM, GSoS, OREO, & LLM frozen; controller \\
      \; (distill / fine-tune) & Agent Q, rStar-Math & is external and tiny \\
      \addlinespace
      Learned process reward & ReST-MCTS*, rStar-Math, & symbolic verifier is \\

      \addlinespace
      Prompt-level RL over & RLPrompt, TEMPERA & multi-step typed ops + \\
      \; frozen LM & & pointer over a forest \\
      \addlinespace
      External module + & PNLC (value only), & discrete action policy, \\
      \; frozen LLM & UniR (logit level) & operation-level $Q$ \\
      \addlinespace
      Context control (concurrent) & Active Context Curation & 7B pruning policy vs \\
      & (2026) & $<$5M lens-as-byproduct \\
      \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
  \end{center}
  \smallskip
  \footnotesize ORM: a learned \emph{outcome} reward model (scores final
  answers); its process-level sibling scores intermediate steps. We forbid
  both.
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- prior art 2
\begin{frame}{Prior art: the three real threats, and what survives}
  \textbf{Threats a reviewer will raise}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item TS-LLM read as ``same loop, different reward source''
    \item ArCHer + GSoS + verifiable-reward RL: ``each ingredient is
          published, the combination is engineering''
    \item Active Context Curation as concurrent work on the lens axis
  \end{enumerate}
  \medskip
  \textbf{What survives: the conjunction, claimed as such}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Tiny \emph{external} controller (neither the LLM nor an LLM-sized critic)
    \item Factored verb + pointer heads over live node ids of a growing forest
    \item Off-policy value-based $Q$-learning as the spine, MCTS strictly additive
    \item Symbolic verifier as the sole reward, no learned value or reward
          model anywhere
    \item The lens property: action selection = context scoping, with local write-back
  \end{itemize}
  \smallskip
  \footnotesize Honest caveat: defensible but thin per-element; claim the
  conjunction, not a new RL algorithm.
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- results 1
\begin{frame}{Rung 1 is live: teacher-as-controller on GSM8K}
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \begin{column}{0.52\textwidth}
      \textbf{Setup}
      \begin{itemize}
        \item gpt-4.1-mini as both controller-teacher and generator
        \item 500 GSM8K problems, depth limit 16, hard token budget
        \item Verifier: SymPy; ground equations only
      \end{itemize}
      \medskip
      \textbf{Headline numbers}
      \begin{itemize}
        \item \alert{70.8\%} solved (354/500)
        \item 3{,}906 decisions; 10 fallbacks (0.26\%)
        \item median 7 turns; full sweep about \$1.50
      \end{itemize}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.44\textwidth}
      \textbf{Verb usage (3{,}906 decisions)}
      \begin{center}
        \footnotesize
        \begin{tabular}{lr}
          \toprule
          \verbword{compute} & 2339 \\
          \verbword{verify} & 1116 \\
          \verbword{commit} & 414 \\
          \verbword{form\_hypothesis} & 16 \\
          \verbword{abandon} & 11 \\
          \verbword{split} & 10 \\
          \bottomrule
        \end{tabular}
      \end{center}
      \medskip
      \footnotesize The verify-iterate loop works: failed checks are
      surfaced as \texttt{(verify failed xN)} and the teacher reformulates
      instead of retrying.
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- honesty
\begin{frame}{Honesty check: the same model does better without us}
  \begin{center}
    \begin{tabular}{lr}
      \toprule
      Same model (gpt-4.1-mini), same 500 problems, same scoring & solved \\
      \midrule
      Direct prompt, free-form step-by-step reasoning & \alert{94.4\%} (472/500) \\
      Inside the deliberation harness & 70.8\% (354/500) \\
      \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
  \end{center}
  \footnotesize One-sided overlap: the harness solved only 3 problems the
  direct prompt missed; the direct prompt solved 121 the harness lost.
  \normalsize
  \begin{block}{Why this is not (yet) a dealbreaker}
    \begin{itemize}
      \item Rung 1's product is \emph{typed decision data}, not benchmark
            accuracy
      \item The harness constrains the generator on purpose; the accuracy
            tax buys a machine-checkable, verifier-vouched trace
      \item The claim lives at the \emph{small}-model frontier: small
            generator + trained controller vs the same small model prompted
            directly. Lose that margin, lose the approach.
    \end{itemize}
  \end{block}
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- results 2
\begin{frame}{Diagnosis: healthy linear skill, collapsed structure}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item The dominant trajectory is \verbword{compute} $\to$
          \verbword{verify} $\to$ \verbword{commit}: correct play, because
          \alert{GSM8K is linearly solvable}
    \item Structural verbs (\verbword{split}, \verbword{abandon}) appear in
          under 1\% of decisions: behavior cloning on this data alone would
          learn a linear-chain policy
    \item This is a \emph{dataset} property, not a system failure: the AND-OR
          forest never earns its keep on problems with no cases
  \end{itemize}
  \begin{block}{The fix: generate our own problems}
    The verifier can \emph{decide} answers (SymPy solves the task), so we can
    generate problems in reverse: choose the case structure and answer set
    first, synthesize the statement around it. Ground truth by construction,
    unlimited scale, difficulty as a curriculum knob. Absolute values,
    piecewise casework, inequalities, ``find all solutions'': each makes
    \verbword{split} and \verbword{abandon} genuinely optimal.
  \end{block}
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- casework
\begin{frame}{Casework: structure fires, and information beat capability}
  \textbf{The dataset did its job} (\texttt{casework-v1}: 300 SymPy-verified
  problems, published with provenance): structural verbs went from
  \alert{0.5\%} of decisions (GSM8K) to \alert{12--23\%}, with
  \verbword{split} in 20/20 probe episodes and the full per-case arc
  (split $\to$ compute $\to$ verify $\to$ commit) in the win set.
  \medskip

  \textbf{Solve rate isolated the bottleneck by experiment:}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item $40\times$ teacher capability (gpt-4.1), \emph{flat} state
          render: 1/20, unchanged from the small teacher
    \item fixed \emph{observation} (tree render, commit-readiness,
          rejection feedback), same small teacher: \alert{$5\times$}
          improvement, wins in 4 of 5 families
  \end{itemize}
  \begin{block}{Why this strengthens the thesis}
    No controller can act on structure the lens erases: the case for
    structured observations. The residual menu-ignoring errors are
    impossible for a pointer head softmaxing over the legal menu; the
    teacher's mistakes are the training signal.
  \end{block}
\end{frame}

  \begin{center}

    \begin{tabular}{@{}lcc@{}}
      \toprule
      Direct prompt, same scoring & Qwen2.5-1.5B (deployment) & gpt-4.1-mini \\

      piecewise (f2a/f2b) & 31 / 15\% & 100\% \\
      word problems (f7) / counting (f8) & 5\% / 5\% & 98\% / 100\% \\
      \midrule
      casework-v1 (240) & \alert{39.2\%} & 100\% \\
      \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
  \end{center}
  \smallskip
  One generator spans 5--75\% on the deployment model while staying
  trivial for the mini tier (harder mixes are a \emph{weighting}
  exercise); dead-case problems are the small model's weak axis (33\% vs
  43\%), exactly what \verbword{abandon} targets.
  \begin{block}{The decisive comparison, now concrete}
    Qwen2.5-1.5B + trained controller must beat \alert{39.2\%} on
    casework-v1. Every teacher-side alternative (prompts, $40\times$
    capability, completion budget, solve-claim steps) measured null; the
    trained controller is the remaining lever.
  \end{block}
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- next steps
\begin{frame}{Next steps}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Design-true collection} (probe running): hosted teacher
          decides, \emph{local} Qwen-1.5B writes all content, so
          trajectories reflect the deployed model's state distribution;
          then the full behavior-cloning collection
    \item \textbf{Phase B}: per-node encoder + pointer head,
          behavior-cloning warm-start on rung-1 trajectories, then
          $Q$-learning fine-tune against the verifier; the probes'
          controller failures (illegal intents, repetition after
          rejection) are its labeled negatives
    \item \textbf{The decisive comparison}: Qwen-1.5B + trained controller
          vs the same model prompted directly; the target is
          \alert{39.2\%} on casework-v1, and the approach succeeds or
          fails on that margin
  \end{enumerate}
  \medskip
  \begin{block}{One-sentence summary}
    Search and scale live in the teacher and the compute knob; what we
    \emph{learn} is a tiny verifier-anchored controller over a frozen LLM.
  \end{block}
\end{frame}
